Hello, I’m

Xingting Wu武星廷

PhD candidate in Design

Swinburne University of Technology · Melbourne

I’m a design researcher and illustrator working at the intersection of human–computer interaction, participatory design, and everyday life.

My PhD explores outdoor engagement in residential aged care. I work with older adults and care staff through crafting, gardening, and nature discovery to understand how design can support meaningful participation.

My wider research explores human–AI interaction, AI in education, and technology adoption. I collaborate with researchers across design, HCI, education, and related fields, connecting interests in creative practice and learning across generations.

Methods I explore

Fieldwork & co-design

Learning with people, in everyday life.

In my doctoral research, I bring together ethnographic fieldwork, conversations, participatory making, and iterative prototyping. I explore how activities and materials can develop with older adults and care staff, through use and adaptation.

Adapting materials through makingDifferent ways to participate
Q methodology

Making room for different viewpoints.

I am interested in how people understand the same technology differently. My collaborative work uses Q methodology to explore art students’ perspectives on generative AI and creative identity, bringing patterns of viewpoints into focus.

Art students, GenAI & creative identity
Experiments & mixed methods

Connecting experiences with patterns.

My collaborative work explores behaviour and perception through experimental studies and mixed methods. This includes human–machine collaboration, AI-supported teaching, and complementary approaches such as PLS-SEM and fsQCA to investigate technology experiences.

Looking across a field of research.

I also explore how reviews and bibliometric approaches can reveal the questions, concepts, and gaps shaping a field. Collaborative work includes game design for older adults, human–AI co-creation, and generative AI in design.

A place to explore.
A story to share.

In Our Outdoor Stories, I’m exploring how a garden map can connect playful plant discovery with a shared indoor display. A walk becomes an invitation to notice, choose, and tell a story.
